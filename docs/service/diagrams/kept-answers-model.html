Kept WorkOS answers: a model check

Each instance of the Ontology service keeps the WorkOS answers for the permissions of a caller, as Permissions of a caller specifies. This TLA+ model checked the earlier blocking-refresh rules (60-second fresh window, 5-minute fallback on WorkOS failure) with 1 Organization, 1 person with 1 role, 1 service user, 2 permissions (P1 and P2), and 2 instances that each keep their own answers and can start cold. WorkOS can fail and recover, and an admin can change the role, remove the membership, delete the application, or remove a scope. The model also tested the permissions claim of a person's token, used with no WorkOS read for up to 300 seconds. The table and In the spec below state how the current stale-while-revalidate rules (60 seconds fresh and 300 seconds usable for memberships and applications, and 300 seconds fresh and 600 seconds usable for roles) change each rule.

The chart

The model has too many states for a full chart, so the chart shows only the shortest path to each rule that breaks for the person, with the permissions claim. "I1" and "I2" are the 2 instances. The red states break a rule.

Rules

Rule In plain words Pre-SWR model Current SWR spec
Fresh5 Fresh: no request decides on a WorkOS fact that is more than 5 minutes old. holds holds for memberships and applications (300 seconds usable); roles stay usable up to 600 seconds (10 minutes)
Revoke5 Revoke: no access more than 5 minutes after a removal in WorkOS. holds holds for memberships and applications (at most 300 seconds); role permission removals take at most 600 seconds (10 minutes)
Grant5 Grant: no refusal more than 5 minutes after a grant in WorkOS. holds holds for memberships and applications (at most 300 seconds); role permission grants take at most 600 seconds (10 minutes)
Revoke60 Revoke when WorkOS answers: no access 60 seconds or more after a removal, while WorkOS answers. breaks (bug 2), only through the permissions claim does not hold: the first request past 60 seconds returns the kept answer at once while refreshing in the background (300 seconds hard expiry for memberships and applications; roles stay fresh 300 seconds and usable up to 600 seconds)
Grant60 Grant when WorkOS answers: no refusal 60 seconds or more after a grant, while WorkOS answers. breaks (bug 3), only through the permissions claim does not hold: the first request past 60 seconds returns the kept answer at once while refreshing in the background (and roles stay fresh 300 seconds and usable up to 600 seconds)
UpToDate Up-to-date when WorkOS answers: no request decides on a fact 60 seconds old or older while WorkOS answers. breaks (bug 1), only through the permissions claim does not hold: stale answers serve immediately within their usable window (300 seconds for memberships and applications; 300 seconds fresh and 600 seconds usable for roles)
NoWorseThanNewest A WorkOS failure never allows what a newer answer on any instance refuses. breaks (bugs 5 and 6), as the spec itself says breaks during a WorkOS failure and while 1 instance serves a stale kept answer before its background refresh finishes, for at most 5 minutes for memberships and applications and at most 10 minutes for roles
ColdDown503 A cold instance with WorkOS down gives 503, never access. breaks (bug 4), only through the permissions claim holds (the service reads no permissions claim)
Only503WhenDown A request gets 503 only when WorkOS is down. holds holds
NoDeadlock The system can always take a next step. holds holds

In the pre-SWR model without the permissions claim, only NoWorseThanNewest broke. Under the current SWR spec without the permissions claim, ColdDown503, Only503WhenDown, and NoDeadlock hold, Fresh5, Revoke5, and Grant5 hold at 5 minutes for memberships and applications and extend to 10 minutes for roles, and UpToDate, Revoke60, Grant60, and NoWorseThanNewest are relaxed by stale-while-revalidate.

Bug paths

  1. Breaks UpToDate.

    1. WorkOS answers. The role grants P1 and P2. Both instances keep nothing.
    2. The person gets a new token. Its permissions claim holds P1 and P2.
    3. 1 minute passes.
    4. The person asks instance 1 for P1. The instance uses the 60-second-old claim and reads nothing from WorkOS. Allowed.

  2. Breaks Revoke60.

    1. WorkOS answers. The role grants P1 and P2.
    2. The person gets a new token with the claim P1 and P2.
    3. An admin removes P2 from the role.
    4. 1 minute passes.
    5. The person asks instance 1 for P2. WorkOS answers, but the instance uses the claim. Allowed 60 seconds after the removal. The claim permits this for up to 300 seconds.

  3. Breaks Grant60.

    1. WorkOS answers. An admin removes P2 from the role.
    2. The person gets a new token with the claim P1 only.
    3. An admin adds P2 to the role again.
    4. 1 minute passes.
    5. The person asks instance 1 for P2. The instance uses the claim. Refused, 60 seconds after the grant.

  4. Breaks ColdDown503.

    1. WorkOS answers. Both instances keep nothing.
    2. The person gets a new token with the claim P1 and P2.
    3. WorkOS fails.
    4. The person asks cold instance 1 for P1. The instance uses the claim. Allowed, not 503.

  5. Breaks NoWorseThanNewest (person).

    1. WorkOS answers. The role grants P1 and P2.
    2. The person asks instance 1 for P1. Instance 1 keeps the membership and the role (P1 and P2). Allowed.
    3. An admin removes P2 from the role.
    4. 1 minute passes.
    5. The person asks instance 2 for P1. Instance 2 reads the role again and keeps P1 only. Allowed.
    6. WorkOS fails.
    7. The person asks instance 1 for P2. Instance 1 uses its 1-minute-old answer. Allowed, but the newer answer on instance 2 refuses P2.

  6. Breaks NoWorseThanNewest (service user).

    1. WorkOS answers. The application has the scopes P1 and P2.
    2. The service user asks instance 1 for P1. Instance 1 keeps the application with P1 and P2. Allowed.
    3. An admin removes the scope P2.
    4. 1 minute passes.
    5. The service user asks instance 2 for P1. Instance 2 keeps P1 only. Allowed.
    6. WorkOS fails.
    7. The service user asks instance 1 for P2. Instance 1 uses its 1-minute-old answer. Allowed, but the newer answer on instance 2 refuses P2.

In the pre-SWR model, bugs 1 to 4 came from 1 rule: a person's permissions claim was used with no WorkOS read for up to 300 seconds. Bugs 5 and 6 show cross-instance skew when 1 instance serves an older kept answer while another instance has read a newer answer from WorkOS.

In the spec

Permissions of a caller reads no permissions claim, so a cold instance with WorkOS down always gives 503 (ColdDown503 holds). The service now uses stale-while-revalidate instead of blocking at 60 seconds. Memberships and applications stay fresh for 60 seconds and usable up to 300 seconds, and roles stay fresh for 300 seconds and usable up to 600 seconds. The first request past the fresh window returns the kept answer at once and starts 1 shared background refresh. So UpToDate, Revoke60, and Grant60 do not hold, Fresh5, Revoke5, and Grant5 extend to 10 minutes for roles, and cross-instance skew (NoWorseThanNewest) lasts at most 5 minutes for memberships and applications and at most 10 minutes for roles.

What the model leaves out

  • The chart and pre-SWR model above do not model stale-while-revalidate background refreshes or the separate 5-minute fresh and 10-minute usable role window.
  • Time moves in whole minutes, and each event happens at a whole minute. The limits in the chart are 1 and 5 minutes, so age 1 is 60 seconds and age 5 is 300 seconds.
  • A request is 1 step. The model has no read in flight, no shared WorkOS call, and no 5-second time limit.
  • WorkOS answers all reads of 1 request, or fails all of them. The model has no request where the membership read works and the role read fails.
  • The model changes the permissions of the 1 role. It does not give the person another role.
  • Each refusal is 1 outcome: 401, 403, and a hidden type are the same. A 429 and a 5xx are both a failure of WorkOS.
  • The person and the service user run in separate configs, because they share no kept key. The service user has no permissions claim.
  • A deleted application does not come back. The model has no client secret, no token expiry, no scope claim, and no token check of part 1.
  • The person gets a token only while WorkOS answers and the membership is active. The claim then holds the permissions of the role.
  • The model has 1 Organization. It has no second Organization, no Ontology owner, and no object security policy.
